# Literature Review

> YouTrack task: DIP-4.

## 1. Scope and method

The review covers four areas that the project depends on: (A) aphasia and reading, (B) aphasia-friendly written information, (C) text simplification with language models and how to evaluate it, and (D) extracting the main content of web pages. Sources were found by searching public indexes (PubMed, publisher and university repositories, ACL Anthology, arXiv, W3C) in October 2026. Type: **S** = scientific (peer-reviewed journal or conference), **O** = other (standard, fact sheet, reference work, preprint).

## 3. Synthesis

**Aphasia and reading.** Aphasia affects understanding of language and reading as well as speaking and writing, and severity varies widely [L1, L2]. Experimental work shows that people with aphasia read more slowly and comprehend less than controls, that text length affects reading time, and that readability formulas and propositional density did not significantly predict comprehension in one study of 75 participants [L9]. Word-level properties (frequency, class, length) influence how readers with aphasia process sentences [L11].

**Aphasia-friendly written information.** Aphasia-friendly formatting appears to improve comprehension of written material, though not everyone prefers it, and illustrations may distract [L4, L5]. Interviews with 40 adults with aphasia show that both content and design characteristics act as barriers or facilitators [L8]. Most participants in one study preferred combined written and auditory presentation [L10]. W3C's supplemental guidance adds patterns for clear language and recommends testing with users [L3].

**Text simplification with LLMs.** General-purpose LLMs perform comparably with dedicated simplification systems at sentence level [L12] and outperform non-LLM methods across lexical, syntactic, sentence and document simplification in a recent preprint [L15]. Automatic metrics are limited, particularly for LLM output, so human evaluation of meaning preservation and simplification edits is needed [L13, L16].

**Main-content extraction.** Open-source tools such as Trafilatura extract main text from pages and are reported to outperform other open-source libraries in the authors' benchmark [L14].

## 4. Gaps and open questions

- Most aphasia-friendly-text studies use printed health brochures or short paragraphs, not arbitrary web pages [L4, L5, L8, L9].
- Evidence on which automatic readability measures, if any, predict comprehension for people with aphasia is limited and partly negative [L9].
- Aphasia-specific web tools exist: CommuniKey offers distraction reduction, resizing, read-aloud and AI summaries [L17, L18], and the earlier HAPPI system offered lexical simplification [L19]. In the sources reviewed, none combines main-content extraction, rewriting at an adjustable reading level, an aphasia-oriented reading view, a documented API and a comprehension-based evaluation. This statement is limited to the sources found; see `04-existing-systems-comparison.md`.
- L6 and L7 were reviewed from abstracts and citing papers only; their full texts should be read before the final report.

## 5. Implications for the project

1. **Do not rely on readability scores alone.** Evaluate comprehension accuracy and reading time with people where possible, plus human judgement of meaning preservation [L9, L13, L16].
2. **Keep texts short.** Length affects reading time [L9], so concise output is a design goal.
3. **Make simplification and display configurable**, because severity differs and not everyone prefers aphasia-friendly formatting [L2, L4, L5, L8].
4. **Treat icons and images as hypotheses to test**, not assumed aids [L5].
5. **Offer text-to-speech as an optional combined-modality aid** [L10].
6. **Include lexical simplification** (frequent, short words) as part of the pipeline [L11].
7. **Use a general-purpose LLM for simplification**, with checks on meaning [L12, L15].
8. **Use an existing extraction library** as the first candidate for main-content extraction, and test it on target pages [L14].
9. **Position the project against CommuniKey** [L17, L18]: its summaries are customisable but reading-level rewriting is not described, so the project should demonstrate level-controlled rewriting and measured comprehension.
10. **Involve people with aphasia or their representatives in evaluation**, because accessible websites need user involvement and readers differ [L3, L6].
